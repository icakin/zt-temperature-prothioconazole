**Figure — Panel A. Growth thermal performance curve.** Measured control growth (points, mean ±
95% CI) against the enzyme- and temperature-constrained model (line). Each enzyme carries a
temperature-dependent turnover and a two-state folding term keyed to its sequence-predicted
melting temperature (TemStaPro); the curve's **shape and high-temperature collapse emerge from
these sequence-predicted enzyme parameters**. Two features are not emergent and are stated as
such: the **optimum position is anchored** to the measured growth optimum (24 °C), because
per-enzyme catalytic optima cannot at present be reliably predicted from sequence; and the
**absolute magnitude is calibrated** (a single global turnover scale, consistent with the
documented low bias of sequence-based kcat predictors). The model is presented as an explanatory
account of the thermal response, not a forecast of unseen temperatures.

### Internal note (not for publication) — why the optimum is anchored, not predicted

We tested whether the optimum could be predicted from sequence rather than anchored, using an
enzyme Topt–Tm gap transferred from an independent organism (E. coli; Li–Engqvist Topt + Leuenberger
meltome; median gap 12.4 °C) applied to Z. tritici's sequence Tm. This reproduces 24 °C — **but a
control shows the result is a global offset, not enzyme-specific information**: replacing every
enzyme's Tm with the single proteome median leaves the predicted optimum unchanged at 24 °C
(median Tm 36.7 °C − gap 12.4 °C ≈ 24 °C). The peak position therefore carries no genome-scale,
sequence-specific content; it is a two-parameter re-parameterisation of the anchor. (Per-enzyme Tm
heterogeneity does genuinely shape the *collapse*, which the same control perturbs.) Additional
caveats surfaced in review of this analysis: fixing the Topt–Tm slope to 1 is not justified by the
weak E. coli regression (r = 0.64); the four sequence-based estimates (16, 18, 24, 28.5 °C; mean
21.6 °C) are neither independent nor centred on 24 °C; and the TemStaPro pseudo-Tm is a threshold-
classifier output, not a validated thermodynamic Tm. Making the *general method* predictive would
require a direct TemStaPro-vs-meltome calibration and frozen, target-blind validation on other
organisms — a separate study. For this paper the optimum is anchored, and that choice is stated
plainly.
